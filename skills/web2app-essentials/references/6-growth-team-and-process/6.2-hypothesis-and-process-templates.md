# Hypothesis and Growth Process Templates

*Distilled from course module 6.*

> The originals are working templates — a Google Doc for hypothesis write-ups and a Google Sheet with roadmap/ICE/rhythm tabs. This page reconstructs both in English as ready-to-use markdown. Copy them into your own Notion/Docs/Sheets.

## Summary

Two artifacts drive the experiment conveyor:

1. **The hypothesis document** — one per experiment. Writing it forces research and an impact calculation before anything is built, becomes the team's synchronization point during the experiment, and accumulates into a knowledge base of learnings afterwards.
2. **The growth process spreadsheet** — three views: an experiment roadmap (backlog + weekly status timeline per experiment), an ICE prioritization sheet, and a rhythm/win-rate monitoring sheet.

Tips for using them:

- Fill the hypothesis doc **before** development starts; the "Results" section is completed after the test ends so the doc doubles as the knowledge-base entry.
- Compute Impact with an experiment calculator (expected ARPU/revenue uplift), not gut feel; state assumptions explicitly and link the model.
- Define the target metric, minimum detectable effect, sample size, and audience/impression criteria **before launch** — this is what prevents premature or unmeasurable tests.
- Review the roadmap weekly (statuses) and the rhythm sheet monthly (launch count and win rate by source/placement).

> **Post-course update (verified August 2026): declare the analysis mode — fixed-horizon or sequential — in the hypothesis doc.**
> The fixed-horizon design below (lock MDE, sample size, and duration;
> no early calls) is no longer the only defensible default. Statsig's
> mSPRT-based sequential testing reports a **1.1% empirical
> false-positive rate across 50,000 A/A tests** while reaching
> significance by half the target duration in **58%** of winning tests,
> at a cost of **≈84% of fixed-horizon power**
> ([Statsig](https://www.statsig.com/blog/sequential-testing-on-statsig),
> Oct 2023), and its 2025 Experimentation Week added an opt-in SPRT mode
> with no peeking penalty that still requires pre-registering the effect
> size from MDE, baseline, and SD
> ([Statsig docs](https://docs.statsig.com/experiments/advanced-setup/sprt)).
> PostHog rebuilt its experiment engine: experiments created from
> January 2025 run **Bayesian analysis by default with a frequentist
> option (added May 2025)**, and Bayesian funnel metrics need at least
> 50 exposures and 5 conversions per variant
> ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/docs/experiments/new-experimentation-engine)).
> Template change: record platform, analysis mode (fixed-horizon vs
> sequential), and method (Bayesian vs frequentist) in the Experiment
> Design section — results produced under different modes are not
> comparable rows in the rhythm sheet, and pre-2025 PostHog results ran
> on a different engine than newer ones.

## Template 1: Hypothesis document

One document per experiment. Page title format: `[Tag] short description` — e.g., `[Web] Meal Plans as 2nd Tier Subscription`, `[US] Stress related questions and feedback`.

### Hypothesis

State it as a falsifiable if/then with the expected metric outcome.

> Example: *If we offer a 2nd subscription for meal plans and remove one-time upsell revenue from the meal plan workbook, we'll generate greater incremental revenue from recurring revenue over time, resulting in higher ARPPU and LTV.*

### Evidence

Highlight the evidence used to validate this hypothesis: qualitative and quantitative measures, or simply examples from top players in the industry.

### Expected Impact

Name the success metric and the expected impact as KPIs (CR% improvement) and new incremental revenue. Link the impact model and list assumptions.

> Example: *If we offer a 2nd tier subscription, accounting for the loss of one-time revenue from our meal plan workbook, we estimate an incremental $1.2M in revenue. See assumptions below. [Link to the impact model]*
>
> Example assumption — *+20% increase in CR:* current upsell conversion is 13%, but fake-door tests indicated 40% of users would enter the 2nd-tier sub funnel; given a better monthly product and observed willingness to pay, we assume a somewhat higher CR for the new subscription.

### Solution

Describe the proposed solution in enough detail that readers make no assumptions about what is and isn't included. Augment the designs; call out every new feature, UI element, and UX flow that supports the hypothesis.

### Design

Figma link (include a screen-flow if there is any transition logic).

### Experiment Design

**Events** — the analytics events the experiment relies on (verify they fire during QA).

**Target metric**

- ARPU
- Metric baseline = ___
- Minimum detectable effect = ___
- Sample size per variation (significance = 95%, power = 80%) = ___

**Secondary metrics**

- Subscription-to-cancel conversion
- Subscription plan shares (plan mix)
- Refund rate

**Test audience and duration**

- *Audience:* customer attributes and platform — country, platform, channel, onboarding data, etc.
- *Impression criteria:* when a customer counts as a test participant for results purposes (e.g., only customers who bought a 1-year plan in onboarding are included).
- *Test duration:*
  - Average traffic of the targeted audience = X users/day
  - Test duration = 2 × sample size per variation ÷ avg daily traffic = X days
  - Planned test timeline: `<start date>` – `<end date>`

### Results

*(Filled in after the test.)*

- Results dashboard (link)
- Test start and stop dates
- **Takeaways:**
  - Verdict: failed / success
  - Key metric changes summary, e.g.:
    - ARPU increased by 5%; test group chose the expensive plan more often
    - Conversion to subscription insignificantly decreased (−5%)
    - Cancel rate decreased by 10.9%
  - Learnings & insights: what did we learn from this experiment? How can we improve?

One row per experiment; one column per week showing the stage the experiment is in that week. Statuses: `Spec/Grooming`, `Spec/Design`, `Discovery`, `Design`, `Development`, `Running`, `Analyse`, `Roll-out`, `Roll-back`, `? Roll-out/Roll-back` (decision pending), `Rolled out`.

Field notes:

- **ID** — stable experiment key (`EXP-NN`), referenced from hypothesis docs and dashboards.
- **Segment** — target user segment (e.g., "Users segment A", "All users").
- **Priority** — `High (mandatory)` / `Medium (optional)` / `Low`.
- **Score** — from the ICE/RICE sheet below.
- **Funnel** — which funnel(s) the experiment targets (`All`, `Funnel 1`, …).
- **PM** — owner.
- Keep a **Backlog** section at the bottom for scored-but-unscheduled ideas, and a "Total points / all managers" summary row to watch load per PM.

## Template 3: ICE prioritization sheet

Score each initiative 1–10 on three axes, then combine (multiply, or sum — be consistent):

| Component | Question |
|---|---|
| Impact | How big is the potential impact? (Calculate expected ARPU/ROAS uplift with an experiment calculator, then map to 1–10.) |
| Confidence | How certain are we it's going to work? (Competitor evidence, research, prior data.) |
| Efforts | How difficult is it to implement? (All effort: dev, design, content production, and running/analyzing the test.) |

Classify each backlog item by **Type**: `Process improvement` / `Feature` / `Experiment` / `Research`.

| ID | Initiative | Type | Impact (1–10) | Confidence (1–10) | Efforts (1–10) | ICE score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXP-01 | Idea 1 | Experiment | 8 | 6 | 7 | … |

## Template 4: Experiment rhythm / results monitoring

Monthly cut of launch volume and win rate, segmented by hypothesis **Source** and funnel **Placement** — this is how you find which idea sources and funnel areas pay off.

| Month | Experiments launched | Win rate | Source | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| May | | | Competitors | Paywall |
| June | | | Pricing | Quiz |
| July | | | Monetization | |
| Aug | | | | |

Suggested dimension values (extend to fit your funnel):

- **Source:** competitors, analytics, user research, advising, previous iterations.
- **Placement:** paywall, pricing, quiz, monetization (upsells), creatives.

Review monthly against the operational KPI (experiments launched per month), and quarterly for win-rate trends per source/placement/PM.

> **Post-course update (verified August 2026): program-level win accounting — discount, replicate, and audit with holdouts.**
> From Booking.com's 2025 experimentation conference (126 practitioners,
> 40 companies): set each experiment's MDE by treating it as a bet —
> stake (traffic × duration) against payoff (lift × reach) — rather than
> a statistics exercise (Bolt); and when summing "wins" for a program
> review, **correct for false discovery rate, because winning effects
> are systematically overestimated**, re-testing big wins before banking
> them (Booking.com, NS International)
> ([recap](https://medium.com/booking-com-data-science/a-recap-of-the-2025-experimentation-conference-at-booking-com-1010acbde2c4),
> Sep 2025). For the cumulative check the rhythm sheet cannot give,
> GrowthBook's holdout feature ships with concrete operating parameters:
> hold **≤5% of traffic** out of all new features, ship for two weeks to
> a quarter, then **freeze changes for 2–4 weeks of clean analysis**
> before reading the cumulative effect
> ([GrowthBook](https://www.growthbook.io/blog/holdouts-in-growthbook),
> Sep 2025). Template change: add a quarterly holdout-readout row to the
> rhythm sheet and an FDR-adjusted wins column next to the raw win rate.
