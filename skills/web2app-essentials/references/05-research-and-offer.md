# 5. Audience research and offer creation

Prepare a brief for one audience segment, entry promise, observable outcome, and testable offer before choosing screens.

## Build the evidence map

| Area | Collect |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | The situation that starts the search for a solution |
| Alternatives and barriers | What people tried, why it failed, and what makes them hesitate |
| Progress | What useful change they want to achieve and see |
| Emotional stakes | Why that change matters to them |
| Proof | Product demonstrations, data, and customer evidence available to support claims |

Use onboarding answers, cancellation reasons, support, first-session behavior, retained versus churned cohorts, search queries, and creative responses. Separate observations from hypotheses.

Interview buyers, non-buyers, early cancelers, retained users, and competitor customers. Ask about actual events:

- What was happening when you started looking?
- What had you tried?
- What made you hesitate before paying?
- What did you expect immediately after purchase?

Study live funnels for their assumed awareness, meaningful questions, feedback, result, offer, and access handoff. A long-running creative is a research lead, not proof of profit.

## Choose the promise and mechanism

Select the primary problem, why the current approach falls short, how the product helps, and the observable destination. Keep one promise consistent through:

```text
Creative → opening → useful questions → evidence/mechanism
→ relevant result → offer → first product action
```

Each transition should explain the next request. An answer earns its place when it changes feedback, a branch, a recommendation, the result, or the first product action. Cosmetic name insertion does not establish personalization.

For example, an available-time answer can select a matching sample session. The result should explain that connection without claiming a broader assessment.

## Define the offer

Complete this statement:

> For [segment] seeking [outcome], the product provides [mechanism/deliverables] over [period], starting with [first useful action]. The customer pays [initial charge], followed by [renewal terms].

Specify included access, why the duration suits the task, what happens after payment, initial and renewal amounts/timing, cancellation, and the actual refund policy/request path.

Compare possible angles on demand, available proof, reliable delivery of first value, and acquisition economics. Start with one coherent angle.

## Choose the experience

| Format | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| Assessment | Answers genuinely change the interpretation or recommendation |
| Plan builder | Constraints can produce a useful plan before product entry |
| Calculator | Inputs, assumptions, and calculations support the displayed result |
| Guided demo | Experiencing the mechanism explains it better than a feature list |
| Direct response | Intent is high and a quiz adds little useful information |

Choose questions for their use, not to reach a target count. Use sourced testimonials and supportable outcomes; show actual processing, genuine prices/deadlines, and guarantees the service will honor.

## Exercise

Write a one-page brief containing the segment and trigger, problem and failed alternative, functional/emotional outcome, entry promise, necessary questions and their uses, result preview, offer and billing terms, first useful action, available evidence, and unsupported claims to exclude.
