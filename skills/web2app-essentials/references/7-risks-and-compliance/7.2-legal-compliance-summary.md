# Legal Compliance Summary: Paywall Requirements

*Distilled from course module 7.*

A consolidated checklist of the criteria a subscription paywall must meet, compiled from the laws and regulations of multiple countries and US states, plus links to the primary sources. Aimed at quiz-to-paywall web funnels selling auto-renewing subscriptions.

## Core Paywall Requirements

### 1. Clear disclosure of auto-renewal

Be upfront with the user about the essential subscription terms. Disclose:

- the amount that will be charged
- the frequency of recurring charges
- the deadline by which the user must act to prevent the next charge
- instructions for canceling the subscription

Place this information **next to the offer details** on the relevant page — not buried elsewhere. (On iOS, the native subscription popup that appears after tapping the subscribe button self-regulates the payment-plan disclosure.)

### 2. Express, informed consent

Obtain **express, informed consent** to the auto-renewing subscription **before charging** the user. The law does not currently specify whether auto-renewal consent must be separate from other agreements, so there are two options:

- **Safest:** a separate consent specifically for auto-renewal — a dedicated checkbox or button ("I understand", "Accept and continue", etc.).
- **Acceptable:** auto-renewal terms folded into the general terms and conditions, which the user accepts via a checkbox or a confirmation button that leads to the payment page.

### 3. Return and refund policy

Publish a clear refund policy with explicit return/refund terms, and make it **visible to users before purchase**.

### 4. Easy cancellation ("click to cancel")

Cancellation must be **as simple as the initial sign-up**. If the user subscribed online in a few clicks, they must be able to cancel online in a few clicks.

### 5. Renewal reminder notices by jurisdiction

| Jurisdiction | Applies to | Reminder timing before renewal |
|--------------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| US — California, Illinois, Florida, Georgia | Subscriptions > 12 months | 30–60 days |
| US — North Carolina | Subscriptions > 60 days | 15–45 days |
| UK, Australia, New Zealand | Subscriptions > 12 months | 30–60 days |
| Canada — Ontario | Subscriptions > 1 month | 30–90 days |
| Canada — Quebec | Subscriptions > 60 days | 60–90 days |
| France | Subscriptions > 12 months | 30–60 days |
| France | Subscriptions > 6 months | 30 days |
| France | Subscriptions > 1 month | 7 days |

> **Post-course update (verified July 2026).** Newer state laws tightened
> this table: California now requires **15–45 days** for initial terms of a
> year or longer (AB 2863, effective July 2025 — see the California section);
> Minnesota requires an **annual** written reminder for *all* ongoing
> subscriptions regardless of term (effective Jan 2025); Utah requires
> 30–60 days' notice for terms over 45 days plus a separate notice at least
> 3 days before a free trial ends (Jan 2025); Massachusetts requires
> pre-renewal notices 5–30 days out for terms over 31 days (Sept 2025);
> New York requires 15–45 days for terms of a year or longer (Nov 2025).
> Apply the strictest rule that covers your audience.

### 6. Promotional-price reminders (California)

**California only:** if the subscription starts with an initial discount period lasting **longer than 31 days**, send the user a reminder **3–21 days** before that discounted period renews at full price.

### 7. Upsells

Legal note on the typical funnel upsell flow: after the user accepts the upsell terms, there must be a **separate payment authorization** — do not charge an upsell on the strength of the original purchase consent alone.

## Key Regulations and Source Documents

### Apple guidelines (for app-linked funnels)

- [Auto-renewable Subscriptions — App Store](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- [In-app purchase — Apple Developer Documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/in-app_purchase)
- [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

Summary of Apple's requirements:

- **Clear communication** — describe exactly what the user gets for the subscription price (e.g., number of issues per month, features included).
- **Visual hierarchy** — present prices and plan options with a clear visual hierarchy so choices are understandable at a glance.
- **Billing amount prominence** — in the purchase flow, the amount that will actually be billed must be the **most prominent pricing element** in the layout.
- **Free trials** — clearly show how long the trial lasts and the price charged once it ends.

### US federal law (FTC)

> **Post-course update (verified July 2026).** The FTC Negative Option Rule
> ("Click to Cancel", finalized Oct 2024) never took effect: the Eighth
> Circuit vacated it in its entirety on **July 8, 2025** (*Custom
> Communications v. FTC*, No. 24-3137), six days before the compliance date,
> on procedural grounds. The FTC restarted rulemaking from scratch with an
> [advance notice of proposed rulemaking in March 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-seeks-public-comment-response-advance-notice-proposed-rulemaking-regarding-negative-option) —
> as of mid-2026 there is **no binding federal click-to-cancel rule**, and a
> replacement is realistically years out.

What still binds sellers at the federal level:

- **ROSCA (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, 15 U.S.C. §§8401–8405)** — for any online negative-option sale: clear and conspicuous disclosure of all material terms **before collecting billing information**, express informed consent before charging, and a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges. The FTC reads "cancellation as easy as sign-up" into ROSCA and litigates it.
- **FTC Act §5** — deceptive UI / dark patterns, hidden terms, obstructive cancellation flows.
- **State auto-renewal laws** (next sections) — post-vacatur, state law is the de facto national compliance ceiling; build to California and you cover most of it.

Enforcement stayed aggressive despite the vacatur:

- **FTC v. Amazon (Prime)** — settled **Sept 25, 2025** for **$2.5B** ($1B civil penalty + $1.5B consumer refunds, the largest FTC civil penalty ever) over deceptive Prime enrollment UI and the obstructive "Iliad" cancellation flow (ROSCA + §5). The injunction terms read like a paywall checklist: clear disclosure of terms before charging, an explicit consent button, cancellation as easy as sign-up.
- **FTC v. Genesis Tech / Growthmind** ([complaint June 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-sues-stop-sprawling-enterprise-operating-unlawful-subscription-schemes); Wisey, Nebula, MadMuscles/Harna, PDF Guru, ~$250M revenue) — the first FTC case aimed squarely at the quiz-to-paywall playbook: quiz personalization → obscured auto-renewal terms → surprise recurring charges → **upsells charged to stored cards while disguised as confirmation steps** → broken or obstructed cancellation. A TRO was granted. Read it as the line between a compliant funnel and an enforcement target: disclose renewal terms at the point of consent, take a separate explicit authorization for every upsell, keep cancellation genuinely easy.

### California law

- **Transparency in Pricing Law (SB 478):** [AG FAQ (PDF)](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/SB%20478%20FAQ%20%28B%29.pdf)
- **Automatic Renewal Law:** Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17602
- **AB 2863 amendments** ("click to cancel", signed Sep 2024, effective **July 2025**): [bill text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2863)

Summary of California requirements:

- **The price a Californian sees is the price they pay** — display the total price including all mandatory fees upfront (sales tax and shipping may be excluded from the advertised price).
- **Clear and conspicuous disclosure** of subscription cost, renewal dates, and cancellation procedure during sign-up. "Clear and conspicuous" is defined as: larger type than surrounding text, or contrasting type/font/color, or set off by symbols or marks that call attention to it.
- **Easy cancellation** through the same platform the user signed up on.
- **Express affirmative consent** (AB 2863, effective July 2025):
  - obtain express affirmative consent to the subscription terms **before charging**
  - **retain proof of consent** for at least 3 years, or 1 year after the contract ends, whichever is longer
- **Notification of upcoming charges:**
  - subscriptions with an initial term of **1 year or longer**: renewal notice **15–45 days** before renewal
  - free trial, gift, or discounted intro period **longer than 31 days**: notice **3–21 days** before it expires
- **General annual reminders** for all subscribers (effective July 2025), which must disclose:
  - the service the subscription applies to
  - the frequency and amount of charges
  - how to cancel

California enforcement is real and layered: the AG issued a consumer alert on
the strengthened law in Sept 2025, the multi-county **California Auto Renewal
Task Force (CART)** of district and city attorneys drives most settlements,
and private class actions under the ARL surged after July 2025 (settlements
from $1.6M to $7.5M).

### Other state auto-renewal laws (post-course update, verified July 2026)

The course covered California in depth; these states have since added or
tightened their own rules. One line each — check the statute when a specific
flow is in question:

- **Minnesota** (Jan 1, 2025, Minn. Stat. §§325G.55–.62): annual reminder for **all** ongoing subscriptions; **no retention/"save" offers during cancellation without prior consent** (unique — a discount-on-cancel flow needs a consent gate here); online cancellation required if the site has subscription management.
- **Utah** (Jan 1, 2025): renewal reminder 30–60 days out for terms over 45 days; separate notice ≥3 days before a free trial ends.
- **Tennessee** (July 1, 2025): affirmative-consent definition arguably requiring separate consent to auto-renewal terms; bans call-only cancellation for online subscriptions; restricts collecting payment details for free trials without opt-in.
- **Massachusetts** (Sept 2, 2025, 940 CMR 38.05): trial disclosures must state the specific cancellation date; same-medium cancellation; pre-renewal notices 5–30 days for terms over 31 days.
- **New York** (Nov 5, 2025, GBL §§527/527-a): affirmative consent before charging; renewal terms shown before collecting billing info; advance notice of material changes including price (5–30 days) with fresh consent or a 14-day cancel-with-prorated-refund; reminders 15–45 days out for terms of a year or longer; multiple easy cancellation channels.
- **Colorado** (SB25-145, main provisions Feb 16, 2026): one-step online cancellation for online enrollees; save offers only with a prominent, continuously visible link straight to cancellation; scope extended to B2B.
- **Virginia** (amendment effective July 1, 2026): cancellation must be at least as easy as sign-up, enforceable under the state consumer-protection act (base ARL in force since July 2024).

## Implementation Checklist for the Paywall

- [ ] Charge amount, billing frequency, and cancellation deadline shown next to the offer
- [ ] Explicit auto-renewal consent (dedicated checkbox/button preferred) captured before charge
- [ ] Consent records stored ≥ 3 years (California AB 2863)
- [ ] Refund policy visible pre-purchase
- [ ] Online cancellation flow no harder than sign-up
- [ ] Cancellation save offers gated: at most one, with a prominent always-visible path to finish cancelling (California, Colorado); none at all without prior consent (Minnesota)
- [ ] Renewal-reminder email automation configured per the jurisdiction table above
- [ ] California promo-period reminder (3–21 days) for discounts longer than 31 days
- [ ] Separate payment authorization step for every upsell
- [ ] Billed amount is the most prominent price on the screen; trial length and post-trial price explicit
