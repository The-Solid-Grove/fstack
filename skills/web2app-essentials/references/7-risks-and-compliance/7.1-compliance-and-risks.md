# Compliance and Risk Management for Web Funnels

*Distilled from course module 7.*

Practical guidance on the four risk categories every quiz-to-paywall web funnel business must manage, when each becomes critical, and concrete playbooks for payment risk, support, taxes, and legal exposure.

## The Four Risk Categories

The bigger the business, the more risks you must actively close. Prioritize in this order:

| # | Risk category | When to act |
|---|---------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Financial risks** (payment processor bans) | Immediately, from day one |
| 2 | **Reputational risks** (reviews, refunds) | Immediately, from day one |
| 3 | **Tax risks** (VAT / sales tax) | Once you hit revenue thresholds |
| 4 | **Legal risks** (laws and regulations) | Primarily for large companies |

## 1. Financial Risks (Payment Processor Bans)

### Key terms

- **Refund** — you voluntarily return the user's money (fully or partially) because they are unhappy with the service or a charge and ask for it back.
- **PayPal Dispute / Claim** — the user files a complaint in PayPal's Resolution Center.
  - A dispute must be resolved within **20 days** without PayPal's involvement.
  - An unresolved dispute escalates to a **claim**, which PayPal itself adjudicates (~10 days).
  - Transactions can be disputed for **180 days** after the transaction date.
- **Chargeback** — the user contests the transaction directly with their bank or card network.
  - Both the processor (Stripe/PayPal/etc.) and the card network (Visa/Mastercard) charge a fee per chargeback.
  - Transactions can be charged back for **120 days** after the transaction date.

### How a chargeback works

1. The cardholder complains to their issuing bank.
2. The issuer forwards the complaint to the acquiring bank.
3. The acquirer forwards it to the merchant and blocks the transaction amount.
4. The merchant either accepts the chargeback and refunds, or contests it by submitting evidence to the acquirer.
5. The acquirer passes the evidence to the issuer.
6. The issuer rules in favor of the cardholder or the merchant.

### Why this matters: monitoring programs

Visa and Mastercard run their own dispute-monitoring programs, and processors (Stripe, PayPal, etc.) layer their own on top. If your chargeback/dispute rate is too high, you face escalating penalties:

- rolling reserves and payout limits
- increased processing fees
- fines
- account termination

**Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program (VAMP)** *(post-course update, verified July 2026: VAMP replaced the VDMP/VFMP programs the course taught on April 1, 2025; enforcement since October 1, 2025)*

- One combined ratio: (fraud reports TC40 + non-fraud disputes TC15) in a month ÷ settled card-not-present transactions in **that same month**.
- You are identified as an Excessive merchant only if you exceed **both** the ratio threshold (≥1.5% in US/EU/Canada/AP since April 1, 2026) *and* a minimum of 1,500 combined fraud + disputes in the month.
- Full thresholds, per-item fees, and what alert services still do (and no longer do) for you: see module 4.2, "Card-network monitoring programs".

**Mastercard Excessive Chargeback Program**

- Again, you must exceed **both** thresholds (count *and* rate) at the same time to enter.
- Dispute rate = disputes in a month / transactions in the **previous** month — beware: a seasonal spike in sales followed by a normal month inflates this ratio.
- Calculated at the start of each month for the previous month.

**PayPal**

- *Internal disputes* — opened inside PayPal (20 days to resolve; unresolved disputes become claims that PayPal decides within ~10 days).
- *External disputes/chargebacks* — opened directly through the user's bank.
- Fees:
  - Standard dispute fee: **$15 per claim** (waived for transactions under $30)
  - High-volume dispute fee: **$30 per claim** if your dispute rate exceeds **1.5%**
  - Chargeback fee: **$20 per chargeback**
- PayPal dispute rate = (sum of claims) / (sum of transactions) over the **last 3 calendar months**.

**Stripe**

- **$15 per dispute** fee.
- Passes through the Visa and Mastercard monitoring programs (no separate scheme of its own).

### How to keep your chargeback rate under control

- Use **chargeback prevention tools** (Chargeback Gurus, Merchanto, and similar alert/deflection services).
- Build a **chargeback-rate monitoring dashboard**:
  - Track chargeback *reasons*, not just counts.
  - Track Visa, Mastercard, and PayPal **separately** — each uses a different formula.
  - Watch out for seasonal sales spikes distorting the rate.
- **Eliminate the root causes**:
  - A clear billing descriptor and recognizable merchant name on the card statement.
  - Invoice/receipt emails for every charge.
  - Make it obvious how and when to unsubscribe.
  - Send subscription-cancellation confirmation emails and renewal reminder emails.
  - Maintain an FAQ, published policies, and a Help/Contact center.

### Fraud monitoring programs

- Visa folded fraud monitoring into VAMP in April 2025 — fraud reports (**TC40**) and disputes now feed the same ratio. Mastercard still runs a separate **Excessive Fraud Merchant** program based on **SAFE reports** (users flagging the transaction as fraudulent to their bank).
- Important: **chargeback alerts do not reduce your fraud rate** — the TC40/SAFE report is filed regardless of whether the chargeback is deflected, and under VAMP fraud TC40s count even when resolved through RDR/CDRN. You must reduce actual fraud signals, not just chargebacks.

## 2. Reputational Risks (Support, Negative Feedback, Refunds)

### Why support is critical for web funnels

- Acquiring users through a web funnel **dramatically increases** support volume.
- ~**80% of tickets** are funnel-driven: cancel my subscription, refund me, or trouble logging into the app after purchase.
- An unhappy customer with an unresolved problem escalates: they file a bank dispute *and* post negative reviews on social media and app stores, hurting conversion.
  - A dispute costs ~**$30 in fees plus the refund** if you lose.
  - It is usually cheaper and simpler to just issue the refund and defuse the negativity.

### How to run support

Core tasks and KPIs:

- **Availability and visibility** — customers must be able to quickly find how to contact you everywhere they might look: in the app, on the site, on social media, on review sites, by phone, inside PayPal, in invoices.
- **Coverage** — support must answer on every channel: email, social DMs, phone, comments, reviews.
- **Response speed** — an angry customer will not wait.

Tooling:

- Any helpdesk software (e.g., **Zendesk**).
- Review-management and brand-mention monitoring tools (e.g., **AppTweak**, **YouScan**).

### Support as a revenue function

- **Churn prevention**
  - Save users who want to cancel: address the complaint, offer a subscription pause/freeze, a discount, or a different plan.
  - Reduce refunds: add friction to the refund flow, offer partial refunds.
- **Upsells through support.**
- **Collecting reviews and lifting store ratings.**
- **Surveys and feedback loops** — bug reports and feature requests routed to the product team.

## 3. Tax Risks (VAT / Sales Tax)

### Where and how much

| Region | Typical rate | Registration | Threshold |
|--------|-------------|--------------|-----------|
| **US** | ~5–7% average (0–10% by state); no federal sales tax | Register in **each state** where you have nexus | ~**$500K/year per state** |
| **Europe (VAT)** | ~20% average (17–25% by country) | Register once in one EU country (OSS) | Low: ~**$10,000/year per country** |
| **Other countries** | ~10–15% typical | Per country | Varies |
| **AU / CA / NZ** | ~10% | Per country | Varies |
| **UK** | 20% | — | — |

### Practical rules

- If you start with US traffic, you can defer tax work for a long time thanks to the high per-state thresholds. European thresholds trigger almost immediately.
- To calculate tax correctly you sometimes need the user's **postal code** (e.g., to determine the specific US state rate). Also store **IP address** and the **card billing address** as evidence of the customer's location.
- Show tax as a **separate line item** at checkout and on the receipt.
- Use tax automation services that handle calculation, filing, and remittance: **Vertex**, **Avalara**, and similar.

## 4. Legal Risks (Laws and Regulations)

### General approach

- Always confirm specifics with lawyers case by case, and consciously decide which risks your company is willing to carry.
- Exposure scales with company stage, size, and geography. A **large business selling in the US carries the highest risk** — law firms there make a business out of class-action lawsuits against subscription companies.
- The main requirement areas that come up:
  - transactional emails and invoices
  - collection of personal and health data
  - cookies and personal-data consent
  - price display rules
  - mandatory Privacy and Refund policies
  - advertising material rules

### Transactional vs. marketing emails

| Marketing emails | Transactional emails |
|------------------|----------------------|
| Require user consent | Do **not** require consent |
| Sell products/services | Contain no selling |
| — | Triggered by technical events: purchase made or canceled, password reset, order status |

### Invoices / receipts

- **Mandatory** for every first transaction.
- Caution: sending an invoice after each **renewal** drops subscription retention and LTV by **20–30%** — send renewal invoices deliberately, not by default.
- A compliant invoice includes:
  - the seller's legal name and address
  - what was purchased (product name)
  - price and subscription duration
  - date and amount of the **next** charge
  - payment method
  - transaction ID (recommended)
  - how to cancel the subscription
  - how to contact support
  - the cancellation deadline to avoid the next charge
  - links to Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, and Refund Policy

### Upcoming-charge (renewal) reminders

- A renewal reminder email **reliably lowers LTV and retention** — the default recommendation is not to send it.
- Send it deliberately when you need to **lower your chargeback rate**.
- It is **mandatory** in these cases:
  - Subscription term of **12 months or longer** → send a reminder **30–40 days** before the charge.
  - Certain US states and geos require reminders even for subscriptions **shorter than 12 months** (check jurisdiction rules).
  - **Trials** → send a reminder **2–10 days** before the first charge.

### Subscription-cancellation emails

- **Mandatory in every case**, regardless of who or what canceled (user, support agent, or automatic cancellation after failed payment).
- Must include:
  - date/time of cancellation
  - the date until which the subscription remains active

### User consent

- **Personal data** — GDPR (Europe), CCPA (California):
  - Consent must state what data is collected, why, how it will be used, and who has access.
  - Use a consent-management platform: **Cookiebot**, **OneTrust**, or similar.
  - For large companies, fines can reach millions of dollars.
- **Health data** — if you collect age, gender, height, weight, or other health-related data (common in quiz funnels), obtain **separate explicit consent**. For some services it is simpler to decline users with certain conditions or under 18.
- **Marketing communications** — separate consent is required before sending marketing emails.

### Paywall and price-display requirements

- Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, and Refund Policy must be present.
- Explicitly state that the user is starting a **subscription** and consents to **recurring charges**:
  - the full price of the intro period **and** subsequent periods must be clear
  - the subscription term must be clear
- These disclosures hurt conversion, so in practice teams use smaller, lighter-colored text. This is a deliberate risk/conversion trade-off — find the balance appropriate to your company's size and risk appetite.

### Advertising creative requirements

- **Non-negotiable:** hold the copyright/licenses for all music, video, and photos of people/actors used in creatives.
- Optional, depending on company stage and legal advice:
  - explicitly labeling the video as an advertisement
  - disclosing that actors, not real users, appear in the video
  - disclosing that AI was used to generate the creative
  - **fake testimonials carry real legal risk**, especially for large companies — avoid or disclose

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Chargeback dashboard live, split by Visa / Mastercard / PayPal, with reason codes
- [ ] Chargeback prevention/alert service connected
- [ ] Clear billing descriptor; invoice email on first charge; cancellation confirmation email always
- [ ] Renewal reminders for 12-month+ subscriptions and trials
- [ ] Support reachable on all channels with fast SLAs; refund-over-dispute policy
- [ ] Tax registrations tracked against US state ($500K) and EU (~$10K) thresholds; tax shown at checkout
- [ ] GDPR/CCPA consent banner (Cookiebot/OneTrust); separate health-data consent in the quiz
- [ ] Paywall discloses recurring billing, full prices, term; T&C / Privacy / Refund policies linked
- [ ] All ad creatives use licensed music/video/actor footage; no fake testimonials
