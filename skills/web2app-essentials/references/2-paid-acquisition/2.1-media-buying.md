# Media Buying for Web Funnels

*Distilled from course module 2.*

## Essential summary

Paid acquisition for web2web funnels runs on one equation: **CAC < LTV** (equivalently, LTV/CAC > 1, tracked as ROAS). CAC decomposes into a chain of multiplicative factors, so it responds to compounding: many small improvements stack into large gains. The core operating model is continuous experimentation on creatives, targeting, bidding, and optimization events — with creative as the single highest-leverage lever, because in modern broad-targeting buying, **the creative IS the targeting**. Media buying (UA) and web funnel growth are coupled: changes in traffic shift funnel CR and LTV, and changes in funnel pricing shift the buyer audience and ad optimization — so both teams must share revenue/ROAS/margin KPIs.

## The core formulas

```
CAC = CPM x 1000 / (CTR x CR to 2nd screen x CR to paywall x CR to checkout x CR to purchase)

LTV = f(Average Order Value, Subscription Retention)
```

To cut CAC: lower CPM, raise CTR, and raise each conversion rate down the funnel (quiz start, paywall view, checkout, purchase). To raise LTV: raise average order value (AOV) and subscription retention.

### Compounding: small wins multiply

Because CAC is a product of factors, simultaneous small improvements multiply:

| Improvement | Effect on CAC |
|---|---|
| CPM -10%, CTR +10%, CR +10% | CAC drops to ~0.74x (−26%) |
| CPM -20%, CTR +20%, CR +20% | CAC drops to ~0.56x (nearly halved) |

10–20% improvements per lever are realistic in practice; run them continuously (the "1.01^365 = 37.8 vs 0.99^365 = 0.03" compounding effect).

## Who influences what

| Team | Levers | Metrics affected |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing (UA) | Channels, creatives, targeting, bids/optimization | Both CAC and LTV — target metric is ROAS |
| Web funnel (growth) | Onboarding, pricing, funnel upsells | CR (hence CAC) and LTV — target metric is also ROAS |
| Product | Product retention, churn prevention, in-product upsells | LTV |

Specifically for funnel experiments:

- **Onboarding storytelling experiments** raise CR.
- **Paywall and pricing experiments** raise ARPU/ROAS (they move CR and LTV simultaneously).
- **Post-paywall experiments** (upsells) raise AOV and LTV.

UA and web growth feed back into each other — traffic mix changes shift CR, plan mix, and LTV; funnel price changes shift the buyer audience, which changes ad optimization and traffic. **Set shared KPIs on revenue, ROAS, and margin across UA and growth.**

## The four levers of media buying

1. **Creative** — the most effective way to move CAC, and simultaneously the most common failure point in marketing (bad creatives). Creative determines who engages, so it is itself targeting.
2. **Targeting** — more relevant ads mean higher CTR and CR; reaching a paying-capable audience raises LTV.
3. **Bidding** — the bid moves CPM; the bid is also a form of targeting (different bids reach different auction segments).
4. **Optimization event** — what you optimize toward is also targeting.

### Creative is targeting

Run **100% broad targeting** — no lookalikes, no interest targeting. Segment the audience through the creative instead:

- Young model in the creative → younger audience.
- Showing overweight bodies → higher-BMI audience.
- Actor working out at home in the video → audience that wants to work out at home.

Different bidding and optimization setups likewise produce different CPM, CTR, CR, and CAC.

## Measuring CAC correctly

Count the **cost of a new paying user**:

- **"New"**: take a cohort of new users and only the ad spend that produced that cohort. Do not mix in old users acquired by other spend.
- **"Paying"**: define the exact moment a site visitor or install becomes a "user", and compute CAC and LTV from that same point.

### CAC anti-patterns (how NOT to measure)

1. **Relying on Blended CAC** — `(all ad costs) / (all new paid users + all new organic users)`. Acceptable only with few channels and near-zero organic. Don't blend channels together or fold organic in. (It is valuable, however, to measure the lift paid channels give to organic, referral, email, and brand traffic.)
2. **Not separating cold vs. warm traffic.** Warm-audience CAC (brand search, email marketing, retargeting) is always lower, warm volume is limited, and you ignore the spend that warmed the audience up in the first place. Classic mistakes: comparing search vs. paid social head-to-head, retargeting vs. prospecting head-to-head, or analyzing Google UAC without splitting Search / Display / YouTube.
3. **Not separating view vs. click attribution and attribution windows.** View and click conversions must be valued differently — their influence on user decision and motivation differs.
4. **Not computing CAC by cohort.** Spend must be matched to the cohorts it generated.
5. **Ignoring channel incrementality.** Ask: how many sales would we lose if we turned the ads off? What does one *additional* sale cost? Different channels, targetings, and creatives have different incrementality. Typical low-incrementality traps: promo codes / coupons / cashback on CPA, brand search. Incrementality can also be positive (some conversions never get attributed to ads at all).

## Measuring and forecasting LTV

- Forecast LTV/retention **by segment**, where each segment has its own characteristic curve shape: traffic channel, geography, gender, customer segment, pricing plan, product category.
- Analyze historical cohorts and average them to learn the LTV/retention curve shape for each segment.

### LTV anti-patterns

- Computing LTV from revenue (ARPU, average check) instead of **profit**.
- Computing LTV via average user lifetime instead of **cohort analysis**.

## Setting the allowable CAC

- The common CAC:LTV ratio is ambiguous — it doesn't bound the LTV calculation period.
- Prefer the **payback window**: the time needed to recoup the money invested in a channel — find n such that `LTV_n = CAC` (n = the period number for the LTV calculation).
- **Available budget affects the allowable CAC.** Example: two plans — v1 monthly subscriptions with CAC $7, v2 annual subscriptions with CAC $11. Which is more profitable to push depends on the constrained budget and payback, not on CAC alone: lower CAC with faster payback recycles a limited budget more times.

## Channels for web2web

Everything that works for app buying also works here:

- Meta (Facebook, Instagram)
- TikTok
- Pinterest, Twitter, Snapchat
- In-app networks (AppLovin, AdColony, Vungle, ironSource, etc.)

Web-only additions:

- Instead of Google UAC: **Google Search, Google Display, Google Demand Gen** (YouTube, Discover, Gmail)
- **Content/native ads**: Taboola, Outbrain
- **CPA and affiliate networks** — web attribution finally makes these measurable

### Channel characteristics

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Meta** | Easiest to start; scales well; strong optimization algorithms; creatives are critical |
| **TikTok** | Volumes can rival Meta; plenty of adult (older) audience; campaigns are less stable; creatives burn out faster and need frequent refresh; creative style differs from Meta |
| **Pinterest / Snapchat / Twitter** | Less volume, weaker optimization algorithms; hard to scale stably |
| **Google** | Volumes comparable to or larger than Meta; Search and Display/Demand Gen must be set up separately; needs dedicated PPC UA specialists |

## Meta playbook

**Targeting and optimization**

- Mostly broad targeting: geo, gender, sometimes age only.
- Creative + optimization event = the real targeting.
- Optimize on **Purchases** (browser pixel events are often enough, without value).
- Mostly **autobid / maximize conversions**; experiment with cost cap and bid cap too.

**Creative testing scheme**

- Test each creative separately. Most popular setup: **1 creative – 1 ad set – 1 campaign** (or several duplicate ad sets/ads with the same creative).
- Phased budget gates:
  1. Spend 1–2x the expected cost per conversion.
     - 0 conversions → stop.
     - Conversions present → spend 3–5x cost per conversion.
       - CAC > target CPA → stop.
       - CAC < target CPA → keep the campaign and move it into scaling.

**Creative scaling scheme**

- Keep winning test campaigns running; make many full copies of them and push the copies through the same testing gates via automated rules.
- Group top performers into batches and load them into **Advantage+ (AAA) campaigns with larger daily budgets** — these campaigns live longer and more stably.

## TikTok playbook

- Buying scheme mirrors Facebook: broad targeting, autobid / maximize conversions, creative tests in separate campaigns, duplicates of winning campaigns, automated stop rules.
- Creatives burn out faster than on Facebook — fast creative refresh and high test volume are essential.
- **TTCC (TikTok Creators Challenge)**: TikTok's native tool for scaling UGC creatives. Free, and yields **10–20x the volume of in-house production**. Flow: advertiser publishes a brief → creators inside CapCut join the challenge and submit creatives → creatives land directly in the ad account.

## Google playbook

**Google Search**

- Easier to launch (if search demand exists).
- Correct conversion tracking and pixel training are critical.
- Budget time and money for the learning phase — it learns and scales gradually.
- The algorithm is sensitive to abrupt changes in campaigns, bids, and conversion rates.
- Automated strategies and bidding work well.

**Google Display / Discovery / Demand Gen**

- Harder to launch than Search.
- Requires active work on creatives and custom audiences.
- Needs even more time and budget for learning.
- Automated strategies and bidding work well.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Track LTV/CAC as ROAS; share revenue/ROAS/margin KPIs between UA and funnel growth teams.
- [ ] Run 100% broad targeting; segment via creatives, bids, and optimization events.
- [ ] Compute CAC per cohort, per channel, separating cold/warm traffic and view/click attribution.
- [ ] Measure channel incrementality before scaling CPA/coupon/brand-search spend.
- [ ] Build LTV curves per segment from historical cohorts, on profit not revenue.
- [ ] Use payback window (not the CAC:LTV ratio) to set allowable CAC, factoring in budget constraints.
- [ ] Meta: 1 creative / 1 ad set / 1 campaign tests with 1–2x then 3–5x CPA budget gates; scale winners into Advantage+ batches.
- [ ] TikTok: refresh creatives constantly; use TTCC for cheap UGC volume.
- [ ] Google: budget for pixel learning; avoid abrupt campaign changes; run Search separately from Display/Demand Gen.
