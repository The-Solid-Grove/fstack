# 2. Does Web2Web fit the product?

Existing revenue shows that some customers will pay. A web funnel also needs a clear promise before purchase, enough retained value to fund acquisition, and reliable delivery.

## Check seven things

| Check | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Demand | Does the person have a reason to start solving this problem now? |
| Value | Can the benefit and mechanism be understood before installing or using the full product? |
| Useful questions | If the funnel asks questions, do answers change feedback, the recommendation, or the product start? |
| First value | Can a buyer reach a useful action soon after payment? |
| Economics | Can realistic net cohort value cover CAC within an affordable payback period? |
| Delivery | Can a verified web purchase reliably grant access to the correct account, with recovery? |
| Operations | Does someone own support, cancellations, refunds, payment risk, and market requirements? |

Mark each **ready**, **uncertain**, or **blocked**, with evidence. This is a planning checklist, not a score that predicts success. A simple funnel can be a good fit; a long quiz is not a requirement.

## Choose the next step

- **Run a controlled test** when the promise, offer, delivery, and loss limit are clear.
- **Research first** when demand, willingness to pay, or retention is mostly assumed.
- **Fix delivery or operations first** when a buyer might be charged without reliable access or support.

Use conservative, base, and optimistic economics rather than relying on a low introductory price or unusually strong renewal assumptions.

## Existing product or new product?

Start with an existing product when its value is easy to explain and its access model supports web buyers. A new product may be justified when the required promise or first-use experience is substantially different, but it adds cost and removes the benefit of existing evidence.

Choose **buy**, **build**, or **hybrid** by time to first test, required flexibility, engineering capacity, and ownership of payment and access failures—not only the initial price.

## Test the smallest useful idea

Prepare one audience, one creative promise, a short clickable journey, a credible preview, and a real offer. Use an honestly labeled interest/waitlist action without payment details, or a small paid test once payment and delivery are ready. The test should answer whether the message, intent, and offer fit together.

Complete this sentence:

> For [audience] facing [problem], the funnel demonstrates [value] and offers [product]. After purchase the buyer can [first useful action]. The channel is viable if [net value and payback condition].
