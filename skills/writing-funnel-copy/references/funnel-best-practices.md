# Funnel Best Practices

Apply the relevant practices when drafting or reviewing copy. Cite their IDs in findings and experiment proposals so the reasoning is traceable. These are decision rules and hypotheses, not a mandatory screen inventory or promises of uplift.

IDs remain stable when a practice is edited or moved; add new IDs without renumbering or reusing existing ones. The [main skill](../SKILL.md) owns the workflow and approvals; the [Funnel Psychology Framework](funnel-psychology-framework.md) explains the strategy and screen-planning methods.

## Product research

### FBP-001

**Ground the product brief.** Establish the actual mechanism, audience and entry situation, advertised promise, offer, access, proof, and limitations. Separate demonstrated capabilities from expected benefits and unresolved assumptions.

### FBP-002

**Research the decision at hand.** For a local edit, investigate the missing fact that affects it; for a new funnel or repositioning, create or update the project's product brief or `PRODUCT_SENSE.md`. Research supplies Pre-work inputs; the screen sequence belongs to the subsequent approved Emotional Arc.

### FBP-003

**Observe current competitors.** Use a small relevant set; record URL, date, market/device, and the observed path from entry to access. Capture only excerpts that change a decision, and reopen a competitor before relying on current pricing or behavior; a code teardown does not prove deployment or conversion.

### FBP-004

**Separate evidence from recommendations.** Write observation → implication for this product → proposed test. Distinguish observed behavior, measured outcomes, and hypotheses; expand research only when it can change the decision.

## Journey and personalization

### FBP-005

**Keep one entry promise.** Connect ad → opening → questions → result → offer → access, including material branches. Make the first action easy and relevant, and resolve promise mismatches before optimizing later screens.

### FBP-006

**Give each question a use.** Ask for information that changes fit, feedback, a recommendation, or a required product action. Explain consequential personal questions; remove inputs whose use cannot be stated.

### FBP-007

**Return value for effort.** Use a relevant insight, real preview, or product-specific explanation before the next substantial request. Explain the paid boundary before asking people to invest effort to unlock a result; generic praise alone is not useful feedback.

### FBP-008

**Personalize the actual result.** Show which meaningful answers changed which content, explain the connection, and support correction where available. Bound recommendations by the real method and uncertainty; a name substitution does not establish fit, and invented scores, diagnoses, earnings, or timelines do not establish value.

### FBP-009

**Match proof to the claim.** Use verified previews, testimonials, reviews, logos, calculations, outcomes, and guarantees at the doubt they address. Support both explicit and implied promises; place limitations beside the claim and mark missing proof as unresolved rather than filling it in.

### FBP-010

**Earn increasing effort.** Introduce sensitive inputs and commitments after their purpose is understood; preserve supported back, edit, skip, and decline paths. Progress indicators reflect the actual journey, including branches; prior effort is not a reason to pressure a purchase.

### FBP-011

**Describe real processing.** Match progress text and duration to work the product actually performs. Present a story sequence as content; decorative animation cannot imply a personalized calculation or analysis that never occurred.

## Rhythm and energy screens

### FBP-034

**Repay every 2–3 questions.** Follow a run of questions with an energy screen that returns reassurance, insight, proof or a visible use of the answers; keep runs to 3 early and 4 later. Use the [cadence rules](funnel-rhythm-template.md#cadence-rules); a deviation needs a reason in the arc.

### FBP-035

**Pass the trust gate right after the first tap.** Place an authority or expert energy screen by screen 3, built from confirmed ratings, scale, awards or credentials. When those facts are missing, use an expert or mechanism screen and leave open proof slots rather than inventing numbers.

### FBP-036

**Link each energy screen to the last answer.** Open by reflecting the answer or the doubt it raises, then show what the product does with it. Generic praise that would fit any answer does not repay the effort.

### FBP-037

**Vary archetypes and climb toward personal proof.** Avoid repeating an archetype back to back; move from trust (authority, reassurance) to understanding (insight, mechanism, comparison) to proof built from their answers (mirror, projection, plan). Each archetype's formula and references are in the [energy screen catalog](energy-screens.md).

### FBP-039

**Build suspense before the reveal.** Close with mirror → loading that names the answer groups actually used → plan reveal → email → paywall (or an email gate before the plan). The paywall repeats the plan; the reveal labels projections as illustrative and uses ranges for body, health or money outcomes.

## Screen copy

### FBP-012

**Give the screen one job.** Connect its dominant message and primary next action to its approved arc role. A quick scan should reveal the subject, value or question, and next action; remove competing ideas that do not serve that decision.

### FBP-013

**Write concrete headlines and sufficient body copy.** Lead with the relevant idea in the audience's language; around six headline words and one or two body sentences are editing aids, not limits. Keep the explanation necessary to understand a result, consequential choice, or disclosure.

### FBP-014

**Make answers intelligible.** Ask one clear thing at a time and use meaningfully distinct options with appropriate uncertainty or neutral choices where supported. The labels and selection behavior must let the person express an answer the product can actually use.

### FBP-015

**Make the CTA predict the next step.** Match the label to the immediate action and destination. A promised sample leads to the sample; a purchase action makes the paid commitment clear before it occurs.

### FBP-016

**Recognize without shaming.** Acknowledge a specific difficulty, explain a plausible cause, and offer an achievable product-supported action. Keep refusal and uncertainty legitimate; avoid diagnoses, humiliation, exaggerated fear, or moral judgments about buying.

### FBP-017

**Use visuals to explain value.** Choose a real interface, sample, comparison, chart, or authentic testimonial for its explanatory job. Keep chart sources, scales, uncertainty, qualifications, and necessary billing text readable; decoration supports the decision rather than competing with it.

## Offers, checkout, and access

### FBP-018

**Connect the offer to demonstrated value.** Recap the relevant result, included product value, and remaining objections; make the initial offer and primary action easy to find. Start from a proven reference structure (FBP-038); plan count, selected default, price, trial, proof placement, and page length remain product-specific hypotheses to test.

### FBP-038

**Clone a proven paywall structure.** Choose one reference paywall whose price model fits the offer, copy its section order, repeated CTAs and sticky elements, and replace every section's content with the product's approved copy and confirmed offer facts. Follow the [paywall blueprints](paywall-blueprints.md#clone-procedure); a reference's prices, timers, discounts and reviews are never evidence for the target.

### FBP-019

**Show actual billing.** State the first charge, its covered period, currency, applicable tax/fees, renewal amount and cadence, and trial terms before commitment. Distinguish recurring from one-time purchases; keep per-day equivalents and savings secondary to billed totals.

### FBP-020

**Support an informed purchase.** Explain auto-renewal, when and how to cancel before the next charge, support, and applicable refund conditions; obtain the required consent before commitment. Keep service-delivery messages separate from marketing permission where applicable.

### FBP-021

**Keep disclosures usable.** Place required terms where they can be read before the purchase action. Sticky controls preserve access to the full page and disclosures with zoomed text and the on-screen keyboard.

### FBP-022

**Use truthful comparisons and urgency.** Verify reference prices, savings math, deadlines, and scarcity. Compare equivalent periods and real alternatives; an evergreen countdown cannot substantiate a limited-time offer.

### FBP-023

**Match checkout to the offer.** Use payment methods actually supported in the target market/device and keep plan, currency, taxes/fees, coupon, and renewal consistent with the paywall. Make errors recoverable, guard against duplicate charges, and show success only after payment confirmation.

### FBP-024

**Keep exit offers optional.** Use checkout-close or further-discount offers only when supported and justified by the product. Verify first-term discount math, unchanged or disclosed renewal terms, and reopened checkout; preserve the ability to leave or cancel.

### FBP-025

**Authorize each upsell.** Give an upsell its own value proposition, explicit price and authorization, and easy decline path. A saved payment method alone does not authorize another charge.

### FBP-026

**Separate recovery from activation.** Treat unpaid checkout abandonment and paid-but-unactivated buyers as different audiences; suppress unpaid-checkout reminders after purchase while preserving appropriate activation and service messages. For marketing reminders, verify permission, preserve unsubscribe, and use a holdout; attributed recovery revenue does not establish incrementality or email's sole contribution.

### FBP-027

**Complete the access handoff.** Explain actual delivery, registration, app/web access, and recovery after purchase. For implementation tasks, use `qa-funnel` to verify the supported path on preview and after an authorized production publish; copy approval alone does not authorize publishing.

### FBP-028

**Verify current requirements for the actual offer.** Before treating final pricing or renewal copy as launch-ready, check current primary sources for the market, product, platform, and effective date. Use [risk and compliance](../../web2app-essentials/references/12-risk-and-compliance.md) for operational review; a generic copy checklist does not settle jurisdiction-specific requirements.

## Measurement and experiments

### FBP-029

**Judge business outcomes with guardrails.** Evaluate changes on purchase, net revenue per landing, CAC/payback, and net cohort value as appropriate. Track refunds, chargebacks, cancellations, activation/access failures, and retention; a higher immediate click rate or attributed offer revenue alone does not establish a better funnel.

### FBP-030

**Name the cohort and denominator.** Compare like-for-like traffic, geography, device, offer, and maturity. Distinguish landing-to-purchase, paywall-to-purchase, and buyer-to-activation rates; use actual screen views rather than treating a prior-screen click as arrival.

### FBP-031

**Treat reach ranges as context.** The following stack-supplied practitioner ranges are planning calibration, not a representative market study or guaranteed targets. Every row uses unique first-screen visitors in the same cohort as its denominator; the ranges describe different funnels, so do not combine endpoints to infer conditional conversion.

| Stage | Share of first-screen visitors |
| --- | --- |
| First screen | 100% |
| Reach second step | 30–60% |
| Reach paywall | 10–20% |
| Reach checkout | 8–15% |
| Purchase | 0.5–5% |

### FBP-032

**Define the experiment before exposure.** Specify audience, assignment/exposure event, hypothesis, one primary metric, guardrails, minimum detectable effect, analysis method, decision rule, and sample/runtime needs. Prioritize a meaningful observed loss and state the mechanism the change is intended to improve.

### FBP-033

**Protect experiment integrity.** Check allocation and tracking before interpreting results; use the planned stopping rule for fixed-horizon tests or a valid sequential method for continuous monitoring. Report measured results separately from proposed uplifts; use the [experimentation lesson](../../web2app-essentials/references/11-experimentation.md) for the analysis workflow.
