# Funnel Psychology Framework

Every screen is a psychological instrument. It either **adds energy** (the visitor feels understood, rewarded, reassured or excited) or **spends it** (the visitor taps, reads, types, decides). A funnel converts when the visitor reaches the paywall with more conviction than when they arrived, and that conviction was **concluded by the visitor from evidence**, not claimed by us.

Work in three artifacts: **Pre-work chooses the promise and the proof; the Emotional Arc places every question and energy screen on the rhythm; screen copy expresses it.** The [main skill](../SKILL.md#workflow-for-a-new-or-redesigned-funnel) owns the approval handoffs. The [rhythm template](funnel-rhythm-template.md) owns the screen order, the [energy screen catalog](energy-screens.md) owns energy-screen formulas and references, and the [paywall blueprints](paywall-blueprints.md) own paywall structure.

## Core model

### Mental fuel

The ad click gives the visitor a small budget of attention. Each screen adds to it or burns it; at zero they leave.

| Adds fuel (give) | Burns fuel (ask) |
| --- | --- |
| The screen confirms what the ad or the previous CTA promised. | Content does not match what brought them here. |
| They see themselves reflected: "that's me", "good choice", "you're in the right place". | A question with no visible use. |
| A surprising, relevant number, comparison or reframe. | Text walls, unclear purpose, complex UI. |
| Proof that others like them succeeded; credible authority. | The screen feels like selling instead of helping. |
| Their own answers come back as something useful (profile, plan, projection). | Personal or payment information arrives as a surprise. |

**The asymmetry:** questions can only spend fuel; energy screens can add it. So a run of questions must be repaid with an energy screen. The default cadence is **2–3 questions, then an energy screen** (maximum 3 in a row in the first half, 4 later). The [rhythm template](funnel-rhythm-template.md#cadence-rules) gives the measured basis and the exceptions.

Fuel is a design notation, not a measurement. In the arc, mark each screen `−` (light ask: one tap), `−−` (heavy ask: multi-select, typing, sensitive or numeric input, email), `+` (give) or `++` (strong give: proof, mirror, plan reveal). Read the line, not a sum.

### The trust gate (screens 1–3)

Before value can load, the visitor silently asks: **Is this a threat? Is it on my side? Is it legitimate?** Answer within the first three screens through evidence, not adjectives:

- **Threat:** recognisable product identity, real ratings, user counts, awards or press; clear data/privacy note on the first question.
- **Ally:** the hook mirrors the ad promise and the first question is about *them*.
- **Rank:** polished visual quality and an authority energy screen ("You're in good hands", "#1 …") when the facts exist.

This is why strong funnels place an **authority burst at screen 2–3**: the visitor has just made the first tap and receives immediate confirmation that they chose well.

### The Hitchcock principle: self-generated value

Hitchcock shows the bomb under the table and then films an ordinary conversation; the audience generates the tension themselves. Apply it to value: **show the evidence and let the visitor conclude the benefit.**

- Don't write "This app is amazing." Show "4.7★ · 10+ years teaching piano · millions of learners" and let them conclude it.
- Don't write "You'll learn fast." Show their own answers mapped to a level bar ("You are HERE"), then the first song they'll play in day 1.
- Don't write "Other methods fail." Show the motivation curve *with the method* vs *other methods*, labelled as illustrative.

**Implementation pattern (a Hitchcock moment):**

1. Present a framing fact (sourced number, mechanism, catalogue size).
2. Ask a question that makes it personal.
3. Reflect the answer back: mirror, calculation or loading that names the inputs.
4. Reveal a bounded result: range, date, plan, "illustrative" label where projected.
5. The visitor fills in the optimistic conclusion. It is theirs, which also protects retention: they own the expectation.

Self-generated value is only as honest as its inputs: use real answers, real product facts and sourced numbers. A missing fact becomes an open slot, never an invented number.

### Investment ladder

Commitment grows in small steps; each step makes the next easier and makes leaving feel like a loss.

1. **Look** — hook screen, near-zero effort.
2. **Tap** — identity, goal, preference (single choice, image cards).
3. **Describe** — situation, experience, pains (single/multi-choice, scale).
4. **Disclose** — behaviour, numbers, sensitive topics (after trust is built; followed by reassurance).
5. **Commit** — "how will you use it" choices, name, email.
6. **Pay** — paywall, after the plan reveal.

Never start hard. Sensitive questions come after the trust gate and are always followed by an answer-linked reassurance.

### System 1 shortcuts, honestly used

The fast-scanning mind lets through what is familiar, what confirms what they believe, and what is genuinely novel. Use: **familiar UI** (progress, cards, checkmarks), **anchoring** (compare price to an everyday item, minutes to hours), **social proof** (people like them), **confirmation** ("Many people your age start here"), **loss framing** (what they keep missing) — all with real facts, and without shaming.

**Poke → soothe → empower:** name a real difficulty lightly, relieve it immediately ("No worries, you're in the right place"), then show an achievable next step. Never leave the visitor in guilt or fear.

### Attention

Visitors scan for about three seconds. The element that takes the most attention must carry the value: a number, a chart, a product visual, their own answers. Faces attract attention but carry value only when the face *is* the message (testimonial, expert). Numbers and icons beat paragraphs.

**3-second test:** if someone glances at the screen and swipes, what single thing stuck? If "nothing" or "a nice picture", rewrite or redesign.

## Pre-work

### 1. Foundation

Fill in short statements; label missing facts and hypotheses instead of inventing them.

| Item | Establish |
| --- | --- |
| Product and mechanism | What the product does, how it works (named method if any), limitations. |
| Audience and situation | Who decides, what they are trying to do, what makes it hard now. |
| Entry promise | Actual ad/referral promise when supplied (otherwise a hypothesis) and what screen 1 must confirm. |
| Offer and access | Free vs paid value, confirmed price/trial/renewal terms, post-purchase path. Unknowns stay open. |
| Brand feeling | One primary tone (and optionally one supporting) for copy. |

### 2. Five-column table

One row per meaningful problem. Connect the columns across the row; use audience language when sourced and mark inferred pains as hypotheses.

| Problems | Why current solutions fail | Barriers to buying | Values (concrete benefits) | Higher-level values (who they become, how they feel) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specific situation and its consequence. | The structural flaw in what they do now. | Doubt, price, "I tried before", time, privacy, scam fear. | A demonstrable thing the product does. | Pride, control, calm, belonging, confidence. |

Choose one **primary anchor**: the problem that matches the audience, entry promise and product best. Order supporting benefits by relevance and strength of evidence. Problems feed the reassurance and insight screens; failures feed comparison screens; barriers feed authority, expert, testimonial and FAQ; values feed mechanism, product preview and the paywall benefit list; higher-level values feed the plan reveal and paywall hero.

### 3. Transformation and entry promise

**From [entry situation and belief] to [credible destination and belief], through [real mechanism].** Separate what the funnel can demonstrate now from what needs product use later.

### 4. Energy inventory

Energy screens need material. List every usable asset and its status; this decides which [energy archetypes](energy-screens.md) the arc can use.

| Asset | Example | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ratings and reviews | Store rating, review count, Trustpilot | confirmed / needs source / unavailable |
| Scale | Users, learners, sessions, years in market | … |
| Awards and press | Editors' Choice, publications | … |
| Authority | Experts, institutions, method authors | … |
| Mechanism | Named method, how it works, what is personalised | … |
| Product visuals | Real UI, catalogue, sample output | … |
| Testimonials | Real quotes with permission | … |
| Statistics | Sourced numbers that reframe the problem | … |

Ask the user for missing high-value assets (rating, user count, method name, catalogue size) in one compact round. Until confirmed, an asset is an open slot in copy: `[PROOF: App Store rating]`.

### 5. Hitchcock moments

Plan 2–3 moments where the visitor concludes value from their own inputs: e.g. level mirror ("You are HERE"), first-result preview ("In 1 day you'll play: …"), projection to a date from their goal, catalogue matched to chosen genres. For each: input → what we reveal → what they conclude → which evidence makes it honest.

### 6. Approval-ready Pre-work

Return: foundation, five-column table, primary anchor and value priority, transformation, entry promise and paid boundary, energy inventory with gaps, Hitchcock moments, open decisions. Keep it reviewable without screen copy; the screen sequence belongs to the arc.

## Emotional Arc

The arc is the plan for **what the visitor feels before each screen, what the screen does to change it, and what they decide next**, laid on the [rhythm template](funnel-rhythm-template.md).

### 1. Feeling journey

One plain line, e.g. **Curious → seen ("that's me") → reassured → excited by what's possible → convinced it's built for me → ready to start.**

### 2. Rhythm strip

Write the whole sequence as a strip so pacing is visible at a glance:

`Q E Q Q Q E Q E Q Q E Q E Q E M L P @ $`

`Q` question · `E` energy screen · `M` profile mirror · `L` loading · `P` plan reveal · `@` email · `$` paywall. Check it against the [cadence rules](funnel-rhythm-template.md#cadence-rules) before writing the table.

### 3. Screen map

| # | Screen | Job · Pre-work link | Before → after | What earns it (evidence / Hitchcock) | Fuel | Reference | Answer use → next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S01 | Q · hook, image choice | Confirm entry promise · P1 | Curious → "this is for me" | Promise headline + one-tap identity question | − | `simply-piano` #1 | Age adapts examples → S02 |
| S02 | E · authority burst | Trust gate · B1 | Unsure it's legit → "I'm in good hands" | `[PROOF: rating]`, `[PROOF: learners]`, years of teaching | ++ | `simply-piano` #2 | → S03 |

- **Screen:** `Q` with the control (single, multi, image, scale, input), or `E` with its [energy archetype](energy-screens.md), or `M`/`L`/`P`/`@`/`$`.
- **Reference:** a funnel/screen from `fgrove references` (or the catalog) whose composition this screen will follow. Design reuses it.
- **Answer use:** every question names what its answer changes (an energy screen, the mirror, the plan, the paywall). Remove questions with no use.

Sentence test for each row: **The visitor thinks [before]. We show [specific evidence], so they feel [after]. We ask [effort] because [use], then [next].**

### 4. Fuel and effort check

Read the fuel column top to bottom: no more than 3 consecutive asks in the first half (4 later), every `−−` followed by a `+` within two screens, a `++` in the first three screens, and the plan reveal (`++`) right before email and paywall. Write 3–5 lines: where effort is spent, where it is repaid, where trust is earned, why the paywall appears there.

### 5. Approval-ready arc

Return: feeling journey, rhythm strip, screen map, fuel check, closing order, chosen [paywall blueprint](paywall-blueprints.md) and reference, material branches, open decisions. Labels stay structural; finished headlines and CTAs belong to the copy stage.

## Screen copy

Expand each approved row with the same ID:

| Field | Write |
| --- | --- |
| ID, screen, archetype | From the arc; energy screens name their archetype and reference. |
| Headline | One idea, audience language, ~6 words target (up to ~10 when needed). Put the key phrase in emphasis. |
| Body | 0–2 short sentences. Replace sentences with a number, icon row or chart where possible. |
| Question and choices | Exact wording, single/multi, 2–6 distinct options (plus "Not sure" where honest), icon/image per option when the reference uses them, and the answer's use. |
| Evidence / visual | The dominant visual and what it proves: chart, rating block, product UI, level bar, catalogue, portrait. Exact source or `[PROOF: …]` slot. |
| CTA | Predicts the next screen ("Continue", "Show my plan", "Get my plan"). Single-choice screens advance on tap. |
| Personalisation | Which earlier answers appear here and how (`{goal}`, `{level}`, `{age}`). |
| Fuel and transition | Fuel mark and what the next screen must fulfil. |

### Writing rules

- **Answer-linked first words.** Energy screens after a question open by reflecting the answer: "Beginner? No worries…", "Great choice!", "At {age}, …".
- **Numbers beat adjectives.** "5000+ songs" beats "huge library"; "4.7★" beats "loved".
- **Questions earn their answer.** Every question visibly unlocks something within the next few screens.
- **One emphasis per screen.** One highlighted phrase or one oversized number.
- **Tone:** confident expert on the visitor's side, never a salesperson. "We'll build it around you", never "Buy now".
- **Ranges and labels:** projections show a range or an "illustrative, based on your answers" note; no guaranteed outcomes in health, finance or similar categories.
- **Loaders name real work:** list the answer groups actually used to build the plan; add proof or a micro-question while it runs.
- **Email states its purpose:** save or send the plan; separate marketing consent when required.
- **Paywall:** follow the [paywall blueprints](paywall-blueprints.md); billed totals, renewal, trial and cancellation terms are readable before the CTA ([offer practices](funnel-best-practices.md#offers-checkout-and-access)).

Copy research owns messaging. Implementation metadata, answer persistence, routing and analytics come from the managed project contract named in the main skill.

## Review checks

Run these before presenting copy (the main skill's self-test). Fix failures, then re-run.

### Per screen

| Check | Pass when | If it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Value | The screen loads one specific value or gets one needed answer. | Cut it or merge it. |
| Fuel | Its fuel mark fits the cadence around it. | Move it, or add/merge an energy screen. |
| Scan | A 3-second glance leaves the intended idea. | Shorten headline; make the visual carry it. |
| Attention | The biggest element carries the value (number, chart, product, their answers). | Swap the dominant element. |
| Hitchcock | The visitor concludes the value from evidence; no bare "amazing/best" claims. | Replace the claim with the proof or their own data. |
| Answer link | Energy screens reflect the preceding answer or the next doubt. | Rewrite the opening words around the answer. |
| Commitment | Effort matches the position on the investment ladder. | Move later or simplify. |
| Trust | Screens 1–3 resolve threat, ally and rank. | Add the authority burst or privacy note. |
| Truth | Every number, rating, testimonial and projection is sourced, labelled or an open slot. | Mark `[PROOF: …]` or remove. |

### Whole journey

- **Promise chain:** ad → hook → questions → energy → mirror → plan → paywall keep one promise; each CTA is fulfilled by the next screen.
- **Rhythm:** the strip passes the [cadence rules](funnel-rhythm-template.md#cadence-rules); archetypes vary and progress from trust to mechanism to personal proof.
- **Hitchcock moments:** each planned moment exists, uses real inputs and lands before the paywall.
- **Paywall parity:** the paywall follows its reference's section order and every section has content or an open slot.

### Rendered check

Copy is not finished until it has been seen on a phone-sized screen. Render it with [preview-funnel](../../preview-funnel/SKILL.md) and inspect every screen at the small first-view size from the [visual pass](../../qa-funnel/references/checklist.md#visual-pass) (`375x548`, what an iPhone SE shows at page load): headline within three lines, single-choice options and the CTA visible without scrolling, energy screens show their evidence block or a labelled visual placeholder, paywall sections in order. Shorten or restructure copy that does not fit, then re-check. Full visual design is [design-funnel](../../design-funnel/SKILL.md), including its [first-three-screen checkpoint](../../design-funnel/SKILL.md#first-three-screen-feedback-checkpoint); built funnels use [qa-funnel](../../qa-funnel/references/design.md).
