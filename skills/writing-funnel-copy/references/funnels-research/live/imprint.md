## Standout Techniques
- **Visual-format differentiation as the core promise** — "knowledge, visualized" + actual visual lesson mockups throughout; the quiz repeatedly demonstrates the mechanism (Active Learning / Sustained Focus / collectible cards) rather than just claiming benefits.
- **Answer-tied reframes:** the "browse social media" answer → "turn your screen time into impactful learning"; the low-reading answer → "it's hard to set aside time… bite-sized lessons make it easy." Each probe's payoff is personalized to the user's own admission.
- **Habit-stacking moment grid** (Bed/Commute/Lunch/Elevator…) — directly raises perceived *ability/feasibility*, the half of motivation most onboardings ignore.
- **"75 bite-sized lessons in a month!"** — converts an abstract "5 min/day" choice into a concrete, impressive self-generated number.
- **Content-rating with real, recognizable titles** ("Flow", "The Science of Happiness") — builds a personalized library, creates investment, and borrows credibility from known works.
- **Award-badge social proof reused at multiple beats** (3 distinct awards) rather than a single trust stamp.
- **Continuous thin progress bar** (not stage-chunked) — keeps the "almost done" feeling without revealing the full length.

## Weaknesses / Risks
- **Account/password gate BEFORE the paywall** — Imprint forces account creation (email + password) before showing pricing. This is a high-friction wall that costs users right at the decision zone, and it blocks any non-committed browser (including this walkthrough) from seeing the offer. Email-only or social sign-in would lower friction; deferring account creation until after the plan is shown would likely convert better.
- **Render lag:** each step blanks then fades in (the page text consistently pre-loads the *next* screen) — on slow connections this reads as jank.
- **Long self-assessment middle:** two consecutive "compared to others" scales risk feeling like filler unless the resulting plan visibly reflects them.
- **Testimonial typo** ("I've take time") in a polished funnel slightly dents credibility.
