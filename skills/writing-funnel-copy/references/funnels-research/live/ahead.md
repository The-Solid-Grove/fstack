## Standout Techniques
- **Therapy/clinical positioning over diet-style hype:** "AI pocket therapist," Oxford/Cambridge/Harvard scientists, deep childhood/parenting/trauma profiling, and a long psychometric Likert battery make the quiz feel like a genuine psychological assessment — building authority and self-disclosure investment.
- **Interactive loader popups:** instead of a passive progress bar, Ahead interrupts the plan-build with 4 Yes/No commitment questions ("Are you ready to feel emotionally stronger?"), keeping engagement high and stacking micro-commitments at the highest-intent moment.
- **Contextual word tags on Likert answers** ("Happy", "Grow") give instant validating feedback, reinforcing the "this understands me" feeling.
- **Polished animated transitions** (each screen fades in) and a calm lavender/illustrated aesthetic match the wellness category and lower perceived sales pressure.
- **Aggressive close-intent downsell** (51%→70%, timer reset, "new promo code") mirrors the fitness funnels.

## Weaknesses / Risks
- **Sensitive data with light framing:** the funnel collects childhood/parenting/"abusive & harmful"/trauma data and detailed mental-health self-assessment, yet the only consent is "*Your email is used to store your plan." For a quasi-clinical product this is a notable privacy/ethics gap.
- **Funnel length:** ~35+ screens with ~12 near-identical emoji-Likert statements risks fatigue; the contextual tags and interstitials only partly offset this.
- **Evergreen-urgency credibility:** the "51% discount reserved for 9:59" timer resets on close and re-issues a deeper "new promo code" (70%), which a savvy user will read as fake scarcity.
- **No money-back guarantee** shown at the paywall — risk objection is left unaddressed beyond FAQ, weaker than the fitness funnels' explicit refund offers.
- **Quarterly rollover surprise:** "€0.36/day" headline masks a €31.99-now → €79.99/3-months recurring charge; terms are in low-contrast fine print.
- **Mental-health outcome claims** ("65% saw dramatic improvements," EI built by elite-university scientists) could attract regulatory/ASA scrutiny if not substantiated.
