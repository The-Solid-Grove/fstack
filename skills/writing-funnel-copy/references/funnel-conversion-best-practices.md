# Web Funnel Conversion Best Practices

Use this reference as a clean catalog of funnel experiments for quiz-to-paywall
and web-to-app flows. Apply only the patterns that match the product, offer,
available proof, and compliance limits.

## Core Principle

Conversion improves when the funnel progressively builds commitment before the
price appears, then removes friction at the payment moment and gives hesitant
users a recovery path.

## Quiz Flow Patterns

### 1. Simplify the First Screen

**Use when:** The first quiz question offers many goals, symptoms, or outcomes.

**Do this:**

- Show a maximum of 5 goal options on the opening quiz question.
- Keep labels short, concrete, and easy to scan.
- Prioritize the 4-5 options most resonant for the target audience.

**Why it works:** Fewer options reduce decision fatigue and make the first tap
feel easy.

**Impact pattern:** Screen conversion +10-15%.

### 2. Add Interstitial Screens Across the Quiz

**Use when:** The quiz feels like a long form or has several consecutive
questions.

**Do this:**

- Insert at least 4 interstitial screens through longer quiz flows.
- Use them for social proof, result previews, motivation, or short value
  reveals.
- Treat interstitials as mini paywalls that warm up intent before the final
  offer.

**Good interstitial content:**

- Compact press or authority proof when available.
- A bold, relevant stat that supports the user's goal.
- A motivational headline with one CTA to continue.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +10-15%.

### 3. Provide Feedback After Each Answer

**Use when:** Users answer a question and immediately move to the next one with
no acknowledgment.

**Do this:**

- Show a brief inline feedback message after selection.
- Acknowledge the user's answer.
- Tie the answer to how the plan or product will adapt.

**Example shape:** "Got it. We will use that to shape your plan around the
moments cravings usually hit hardest."

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +5%.

### 4. Use Adaptive Personalized Feedback

**Use when:** Multiple answer choices represent meaningfully different user
situations.

**Do this:**

- Branch the feedback copy based on the selected answer.
- Mirror the user's exact situation instead of using generic praise.
- Use different headings for different user states, such as beginner,
  experienced, returning, stuck, or ambitious.

**Why it works:** Specific feedback makes the product feel observant and
personal.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +7%.

### 5. Showcase Visual Progress During the Quiz

**Use when:** The product can credibly project progress, milestones, or a future
state from quiz inputs.

**Do this:**

- At a milestone point, show a visual progress summary.
- Use a chart, curve, timeline, score, or before/after meter.
- Connect the user's inputs to the forecast without overpromising.

**Example shape:** "Based on your answers, your first visible milestone could
arrive in 28 days" with a personalized curve from now to the target date.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +15%.

**Compliance note:** For health, fitness, finance, legal, or medical products,
avoid guaranteed results. Use ranges, estimates, eligibility language, and clear
assumptions.

### 6. Add a Personalization Question on the Loader

**Use when:** The funnel has an analysis, matching, or plan-building loader.

**Do this:**

- Add one short secondary question during the loader.
- Keep it yes/no or one-tap.
- Use it to capture a preference that can personalize the next screen or offer.

**Example shape:** "Want us to include quick meal ideas in your plan?"

**Why it works:** It turns dead time into engagement and makes the result feel
more custom.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +3%.

### 7. Use Authority Proof Carefully

**Use when:** The product relies on science, expertise, safety, or credibility.

**Do this:**

- Show named experts, credentials, methodology, or institution logos only when
  the association is real and permitted.
- Keep the block compact.
- Explain what the authority supports: method, content, review, or research.

**Why it works:** Recognized authority lowers risk perception in health,
finance, wellness, and high-trust verticals.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +2%.

## Pre-Paywall Warmup Patterns

### 8. Gifts, Promotions, and Coupon Reveals

**Use when:** The offer includes a legitimate discount or bonus.

**Do this:**

- Let users actively reveal the reward through a scratch card, spin, reveal
  card, or other simple interaction.
- Make the reward feel earned.
- Carry the reward into the paywall with the discount pre-applied and visible.

**Scratch-card flow:**

1. Show a branded "Reveal your deal" card.
2. User scratches or taps to reveal the discount.
3. Redirect to paywall with the promo applied and timer visible.

**Wheel flow:**

1. Show a prize wheel with believable offer options.
2. User taps to spin.
3. Celebration screen confirms the unlocked reward.
4. CTA moves to the paywall with the reward applied.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +8%.

### 9. Commitment Screen

**Use when:** The product supports a personal goal that benefits from
commitment, especially fitness, health, learning, or behavior change.

**Do this:**

- Ask users to confirm commitment before the paywall.
- Use a digital signature, pledge, goal confirmation, or "I commit" action.
- Keep the tone empowering rather than shaming.

**Why it works:** Active commitment increases follow-through through
consistency bias.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +7-11%.

## Paywall and Checkout Experiments

### 10. Place Apple Pay / Google Pay Under Price Options

**Use when:** One-tap wallet payment is available.

**Do this:**

- Put Apple Pay or Google Pay directly below the pricing tiers.
- Put card payment or the standard CTA below the wallet option.
- Keep the distance between pricing decision and payment action minimal.

**Why it works:** Wallet payments remove card-entry friction at the highest
intent moment.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +10-15%.

### 11. Visualize the User's Future State on the Paywall

**Use when:** Quiz answers can power a credible future-state visualization.

**Do this:**

- Put a personalized projection near the top of the paywall.
- Use a goal chart, before/after widget, skill meter, or progress bars.
- Include the user's selected target, timeframe, or outcome when available.

**Why it works:** It keeps the user focused on the result, not the spend.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +10-15%.

### 12. Use a Timer + Discount Banner

**Use when:** The discount or bonus is real and time-limited.

**Do this:**

- Add a sticky top banner with discount and countdown.
- Include a CTA that scrolls to or opens checkout.
- Keep the offer visible while users review the paywall.

**Why it works:** Honest urgency keeps the offer salient throughout the page.

**Impact pattern:** Subscription conversion +10%.

### 13. Use Checkout Pop-Up Down-Sells

**Use when:** Users can open checkout from the paywall and may hesitate on
price.

**Do this:**

- Open checkout as an in-page modal rather than navigating away.
- If the user closes checkout, show one additional limited offer.
- Keep the user on the paywall so they can re-select and complete purchase.

**Recovery flow:**

1. User taps CTA and checkout opens in a modal.
2. User closes checkout.
3. A limited-offer modal presents a deeper discount or bonus.
4. User re-selects a plan and completes purchase.

**Impact pattern:** ARPU +15%.

## Key Principles

- **Build commitment progressively.** Feedback, progress visualization,
  adaptive copy, and commitment screens increase investment before price.
- **Remove payment friction.** Wallet payments, pre-applied discounts, and
  direct checkout CTAs reduce the gap between intent and purchase.
- **Use recovery layers.** Checkout-close offers can recover users who reached
  the paywall but hesitated.
- **Personalization beats persuasion.** Adaptive feedback, named goals, target
  dates, and personalized discounts outperform generic persuasion.
- **Keep claims honest.** Only use authority, expert, result, discount, timer,
  and guarantee claims when they are real and allowed.
