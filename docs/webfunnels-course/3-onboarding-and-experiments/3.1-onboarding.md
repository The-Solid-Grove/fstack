# Onboarding Design for Web Funnels

*Distilled from: «Курс по веб воронкам. 3.1 - Онбординг» (course module 3)*

## Summary

The onboarding is the quiz/storytelling section of a web-to-web funnel that sits between the ad creative and the paywall. Its job is not to collect data — it is to sell. A well-built onboarding continues the promise made in the creative, builds trust, and sets a personalized expectation that the product will solve the user's specific problem. The canonical structure is:

**Welcome → Quiz/Storytelling → Loader → Sign up (email) → Paywall → Download app**

The lecture covers the selling-narrative strategy (engagement, trust, personalization, feedback loops, value teasers, loaders, social proof, email capture, paywall composition), the post-purchase handoff into the app, and common anti-patterns.

## Goals of onboarding

- **Engage** the user as a direct continuation of the ad creative.
- **Build trust** with the user.
- **Set the right expectation** that the product effectively solves *their* task — each user's expectation may differ, so personalization matters.

## Getting started: two viable strategies

1. **Copy a competitor's funnel entirely** (fastest path to a working baseline).
2. **Design your own onboarding:**
   - Study common patterns across similar products; outline the full scenario as bullet-point theses.
   - Generate quiz questions and content with ChatGPT.
   - Drop the generated content into the standard base structure.

## The storytelling ladder

Build the narrative as an escalating sequence — each stage earns the next:

| Step | Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engage | Easy, low-friction start tied to the creative |
| 2 | Build trust | Facts, proof, credibility |
| 3 | Understand goals | Ask what the user wants to achieve |
| 4 | Understand pains & context | Dig into their situation |
| 5 | Give feedback | React to their answers |
| 6 | Show expected result | Visualize the outcome they'll get |
| 7 | Solution sneak peek | Tease the solution — **don't reveal all of it** |
| 8 | Personalized offer | Present upcoming results as a tailored offer |

Key storytelling principles:

- **Two approaches:** Conversational (quiz) or Show (demo the features). Quizzes dominate.
- **Psychology:** if the purchase is emotional, the quiz has large conversion-growth potential. This is why utility apps sell poorly through quiz funnels.
- **One screen — one message.** Never stack multiple ideas on a single screen.
- **One consistent narrative** across Creative → first screen & quiz → Paywall. Any mismatch breaks trust.

## Conversion-lifting mechanics

| Mechanic | What it is | Measured impact |
|---|---|---|
| Light engaging start | Open with an easy question tied to the creative (e.g., a loading screen combined with a question) | +10–15% CR to purchase |
| Score questions | Situational questions where users recognize themselves | +10–15% CR to purchase |
| Big storytelling loaders | 5–7 slides over 30–40 seconds at the end of the quiz | +15–20% CR to purchase |
| Social proof on action screens | Testimonials placed on the screen where the user must act | +44% CR paywall-to-purchase |
| "Try the solution" interactions | Selfie capture, palm scan, etc. | Up to 80% CR on that step |

### Building trust

- Facts and research data.
- User stories and feedback/testimonials.
- Media features, awards, user counts.

### Personalization

- **Relevant questions and content are the best personalization.** The right questions surface goals and pains more deeply.
- Find relevant questions via JTBD analysis, user reviews, and user research.
- Personalize by audience: gender, age, tasks, use cases. Variants can differ in both visual styling and content.

### Feedback loops

- Dedicated feedback steps that react to the user's answers ("Great — people like you achieved X").
- Instant inline feedback shown immediately when a question is answered.

### Value sneak peek

Show a taste of the outcome without giving everything away:

- Predicted weight-loss timeline (health funnels).
- Tailored style preview (fashion funnels).
- Assessed language level (education funnels).
- Interactive "try the solution" steps (selfie, palm scan) — these convert around 80% on the step.

### Warming loaders

- **Simple loaders** — mid-onboarding, to vary the quiz rhythm and create a feeling of personalization.
- **Segmented loaders** — end of onboarding, framed as "preparing your overall result."
- **Questions on the loader** — keep the user engaged while "processing."
- **Big storytelling loaders** — 5–7 slides, 30–40 seconds total; worth +15–20% CR to purchase.

### Social proof

- As a dedicated step early or deeper in the funnel to motivate continuing.
- On action screens (paywall, checkout) to motivate the specific action — measured at +44% CR paywall-to-purchase.

## Sign up and email capture

**Why ask for email:**

- Email sequences can drive up to **30% of revenue**.
- Extra warm-up before purchase (raises conversion).

**How to ask:**

- Frame it as value delivery: "Enter your email to get your result."
- Offer Sign in with Apple/Facebook/Google — simpler for users and yields valid emails.

**Where to ask:**

- Standard placement: after the final loader, right before showing the personalized result (Questions → Loader → Email → Result).
- Can be moved earlier in the funnel if it tests well.

## Paywall composition

Benchmark: **10–15% conversion to purchase** from the paywall.

Checklist of elements:

- Personalization based on quiz answers (e.g., "for women 45+ with an hourglass figure").
- Expected result visualization (personal plan, charts, before/after).
- FOMO and urgency (timer, limited-time offer).
- Default-selected price option.
- Reviews and social proof.
- Money-back guarantee.
- Security badges and contact info.

## Post-purchase flow: getting users into the app

Benchmark: **80–90% conversion to app install** after purchase. Three matching strategies:

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Auto-login via deeplink** (download page → deeplink + access parameter → app) | No passwords, no login screen in the app | Deeplinks don't always fire; user may leave and lose the deeplink |
| **Magic link via email** | No passwords, easy flow | Requires a login step in the app and backend logic; invalid email = no magic link |
| **Account creation inside the funnel** | User definitely has credentials | Extra login step in the app, extra account-creation step and logic in the funnel |

## Anti-patterns (what not to do)

- ❌ "Let's just make the onboarding shorter" (or longer) as a goal.
  ✅ The goal is *enough relevant questions and content* for the specific audience — length follows relevance.
- ❌ Optimizing conversion to *reaching the paywall*.
  ✅ Focus on conversion to *purchase*.
- ❌ Polishing UI, debating images vs. text.
  ✅ Put the focus on messages and meaning.
- ❌ Making the user's path too easy.
  ✅ **Positive friction** (entering an email, taking a selfie, scanning a palm) increases conversion — effort creates investment.
