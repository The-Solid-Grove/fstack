# 4.1 Monetization and Paywalls

*Distilled from: "Курс по веб воронкам. 4.1 — монетизация и пейволы (первый поток)" (course module 4).*

## Essential summary

Monetization in a web funnel comes down to one inequality:

> **value + motivation to buy > price + friction of buying**

You can push on all four levers: increase perceived value, amplify the desire to buy (mostly done in onboarding), find the optimal price, and remove barriers at checkout. Paywall work is a continuous experimentation program — on UI/UX, plan configuration, checkout, and post-purchase upsells — and every pricing experiment must be judged by its effect on **ROAS (CR × LTV)**, never on conversion rate or LTV alone.

## The monetization equation — four levers

| Lever | What to do |
|---|---|
| Increase value | Understand what users actually pay for; communicate the product's value clearly; build brand awareness and trust |
| Amplify motivation | Know the user's goals and pains and show how the product solves them; address both emotional and functional buying motives (this is the onboarding lecture's material applied at the paywall) |
| Optimize price | Find willingness to pay; copy market/competitor pricing from popular funnels as a starting point; run A/B price tests; apply pricing psychology |
| Remove friction | Simplify checkout UX; lower the price threshold (intro offers, trials, discounts) |

### Understanding value

Value = the price a user is willing to pay. It splits into:

- **Base value** — the core function the product is bought for (for a phone: calls, messaging, internet).
- **Added value** — functional (better camera, app store), emotional (beautiful design, prestige), social (belonging to a community, e.g. Apple users).

Research methods to quantify value:

- **MaxDiff study** — identify the most important features: (1) list candidate features users might pay for (from surveys and analysis of your current audience), (2) survey users to pick the single most and least important item per set, (3) score and rank feature importance.
- **Willingness-to-pay study** — e.g. the **Van Westendorp price sensitivity meter** for finding acceptable price ranges.
- **Onboarding questions** about readiness to pay.
- **Exit interviews** with users who cancelled.

### Amplifying desire to buy

Traffic and creatives bring users who already have an initial need; onboarding's job is to strengthen that desire before the paywall. Apply the onboarding techniques (goals, pains, personalization, emotional + functional framing).

### Simplifying the purchase

- Clean, minimal checkout UX: easy payment-data entry (Apple Pay or PayPal beats manual card entry), no unnecessary fields, mobile-friendly layout.
- Must render and work correctly inside Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok in-app webviews.
- Build trust: security icons, trust badges, money-back guarantee.
- The lower the price, the easier the purchase: intro offers, trials, discounts.
- Pricing psychology: per-day prices, anchor plans and decoy options, prices ending in .99, etc.

## What to test on paywalls

### UI / design

- **The core value proposition** — what you're selling, on the paywall and pre-paywall, and what the plan includes. Match monetization to the offer (e.g. Noom picks subscription length to match the user's predicted weight-loss timeline).
- Short paywall vs. long paywall.
- Urgency and benefit: discount/promo code, countdown timer, gifts/bonuses.
- CTA button variants.
- Trust elements, two flavors:
  - "I'm not being scammed": 100% money-back guarantee, payment-network logos.
  - "I'm not alone": social proof — e.g. a live-updating feed of emails of people who just bought.
- Proven value-prop patterns: discount with countdown, transformation plan, before/after, weight graph, "what's included" list, free gift.

### Plans and pricing

- Plan configuration: number of options, layout, order.
- **Three plans is the most popular and effective configuration** — choice psychology does the work.
- Which plan is selected by default (matters a lot).
- Plan order and labels/names ("1 month" vs. "4 weeks").
- Full price vs. price per day/week/month; whether the full price and the full savings are visible.
- Intro offers, trials vs. subscriptions, one-time purchases.

### Intro offers (the current default pattern)

Most web funnels use intro offers: **50–70% discount on the first transaction**, subsequent renewals at full price.

- Higher conversion to subscription because of the lower entry price.
- Caution: if the discount terms are unclear, expect a wave of refunds at first renewal.
- The bigger the gap between intro and full price, the higher the drop-off after renewal.

### Trials

Trials generally perform worse than intro offers (though Noom still buys traffic to trials at scale):

- High refund rate; many `insufficient_funds` / `do_not_honour` declines at conversion.
- **Make the trial paid, at least $1** — filters out low-quality users.
- You can build a prediction model and send only predicted-quality conversions to ad networks for traffic optimization.
- Trials work well as a **winback offer**, especially via CRM/email for users who left an email but didn't buy.

### Winback / exit discounts

Extra discount for hesitating users:

- It **cannibalizes full-price purchases** from the main paywall.
- Only works if you can accurately identify users who would *not* buy at full price and show the discount at exactly the right moment.

### Checkout

- Checkout on the paywall vs. a separate screen.
- Payment methods offered and the default method.
- Observed payment-method shares: **Apple Pay 60–80%, PayPal 10–20%, cards 10–20%, Google Pay <3%**.

Checkout recommendations:

- Make **Apple Pay the default method**; you can check via API whether the user has cards linked to Apple Pay.
- Focus the UI on the default method — e.g. an Apple Pay button instead of a generic CTA.
- Add trust icons.

### Post-purchase monetization

**Upsells** — 20–30% of new subscribers will buy an upsell on top; the higher the subscription price, the higher the upsell conversion.

- What to sell: PDF workbooks, extra in-app content, webinars, physical goods, 1:1 coaching, masterminds.
- How: **one-click purchase** (card details already captured), as a one-time purchase or a second subscription.
- Tying the upsell to the ad creative's theme raises conversion.

**Upgrades** — replacing the purchased subscription with a longer, more expensive one.

- Sell it not as a discount but as a longer commitment with better odds of reaching the goal.
- Use cascading upgrades: 1m → 3m, then 3m → 6m or 12m.
- Careful with retention forecasting: the most motivated users upgrade out of the original cohort, dragging down that cohort's retention and LTV.

## Evaluating pricing experiments

- **The one metric that matters is ROAS.** The experiment must grow CR × LTV — never celebrate a CR increase or an LTV increase in isolation.
- Before launching, forecast: price changes, CR to subscription, plan-mix distribution, CR to upsells/upgrades, subscription retention, refund rate — then compute the projected ROAS change.
- Reading results: CVR and plan-mix changes are visible immediately; subscription retention takes time (the longer the plan, the longer you wait for a real answer).
- **Decide faster by predicting retention**: predict the retention slope from the **cancel rate in the first X hours/days** — it depends on the billed amount and user attributes (demographics etc.), so no need to wait for actual renewal transactions.
- Use a spreadsheet calculator: enter assumptions for each metric shift and compute the resulting ROAS delta (course calculator: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1shZQqSUHrEQouG2YEFgPuKFT_Qxi6p8fXiUj-aZckww/edit?usp=sharing).

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Value proposition on the paywall matches the pain surfaced in onboarding and the ad creative
- [ ] 3-plan configuration with a deliberate default and order
- [ ] Intro offer (50–70% off first cycle) with clear renewal terms
- [ ] Apple Pay as default payment method, one-tap checkout, trust icons
- [ ] Countdown/discount urgency element tested
- [ ] Post-purchase upsell and cascading upgrade flow in place
- [ ] Every price test evaluated on forecasted and measured ROAS (CR × LTV), with early cancel-rate-based retention prediction
